## The ONE idea

One dataset can hand you four different true numbers. Which one you quote is the whole argument, and
the decimal point on it does the persuading. Nobody lies; everybody picks.

**Slug drift note:** the folder is `two-numbers-one-report` and was previously titled
*One Number, Four Addresses* — they never matched. Rev 4 title: *Pick a Number, Any Number*.
All metadata, `ClaudeTitleOutro.props.title`, and the outro narration now use this title verbatim.

## What changed and why it's better

Rev 3 had one party making a pick (the vendor, 97.5% not 65%). Rev 4 shows two parties making the
same kind of pick from two different documents — the report (Best quotes 40% instead of the 13.8%
or 21.9% that describe Substack) and the study (Pangram quotes 97.5% instead of 65%). The rhyme is
the argument: selection is structural, not personal. And because Substack and Best are now named
(Honesty Law 3a), the companion "follow the link" instruction is something the viewer can
actually obey.

## HONESTY LAW — this film is about citation, so its own citations are the test

Four rules, binding on the script step.

1. **The identification is an inference and must be labelled as one.** The vendor's page names the
   study by institution and month, not by authors, title or DOI. The match rests on four converging
   details — same university, same month, four tools compared, and the two quoted figures matching
   that paper's table exactly. **If the page still does not name the paper on build day, B09 says
   on screen that we matched it rather than asserting it.** If the page has since added a citation,
   say that instead. Never narrate the identification as given.

2. **Do not claim it is the only source.** The vendor's page cites **three** academic studies plus
   three commercial reviews. This paper is one of them — the newest, and the only one on
   higher-education writing. The film must say so.

3a. **Name the publishers.** Substack, Chris Best's post "Against Claudefishing" (21 July 2026),
    and Pangram's third-party evaluations page are signed public corporate publications. Name them.
    A film about qualifiers falling off in transit cannot itself strip the identifying qualifier
    from its own citations — and B13/B14's "follow the link" is an instruction the viewer must
    be able to obey.

3b. **Do not name the paper, its authors, or its university.** Honesty Law 1 stands: the vendor
    page cites the study by institution and month with no DOI, authors or title, so the match is
    an INFERENCE and is labelled as one on screen at B09. The paper's authors are not the accused.

    *The asymmetry is the point:* name the parties making public claims; withhold the name where
    the evidence is an inference.

4. **Give the paper its due.** It is preregistered, its data and prompts are on OSF, its
   limitations section is candid, and its own recommendation is the film's verdict. B01 and B07
   exist to make that unmistakable.
