## Glossary

**active observation** — The Scaffolding-stage supervision posture: present and ready to intervene, but intervening only when necessary.

**belonging** — The intern's experienced sense that her presence is expected and her contributions count; observable in participation behavior, and distinct from granted inclusion.

**cognitive apprenticeship** — The training model (Collins, Brown & Newman) in which a mentor externalizes tacit reasoning through three stages: Modeling, Scaffolding, and Fading.

**exit interview** — The structured closing conversation in three forward-pointing movements — growth review, career discussion, network conversation — distinct from a performance review.

**FAIR standard** — Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable; applied at the bench, the test that a successor could pick up the work without the intern present.

**intellectual inclusion** — The practice of inviting the intern into the interpretive and design work of science — asking for her interpretation before offering your own.

**mentorship compact** — A written, co-drafted statement of mutual expectations covering timeline, communication, and documentation; a living document, not a legal instrument.

**no-fault error policy** — The explicit rule that immediate, honest error reporting is never punished, so that the details needed to prevent recurrence are not concealed.

**protocol drift** — The compounding of small, unnarrated technical errors when an intern reproduces gestures without the reasoning behind them.

**psychological safety** — The shared confidence that admitting error or asking a naive question will not be punished or ridiculed; the precondition for honest reporting.

**recognition** — The third component of science identity (after competence and performance): being seen as a researcher by people who matter — the component the mentor controls.

**research identity** — The intern's self-understanding as someone who does science rather than someone who executes tasks; its stall in months 2–4 is the failure Module 3 prevents.

**scaffolding** — Support that lets a novice accomplish what she could not manage alone, deliberately removed as competence develops (Wood, Bruner & Ross; Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development).

**structural exclusion** — Exclusion with no identifiable actor, built into how the lab routinely operates — decisions made where some cannot attend, vocabulary that presumes a shared background.

**supervised judgment** — The right independence goal for a six-month undergraduate: the intern decides, and the decision is reviewed at an agreed checkpoint rather than pre-approved.

**timeline budget** — The Day-1 allocation of the internship's months (a 2:3:1 default of training, data generation, synthesis), protecting the blocks most often raided.
